A CRM is only as good as the way it's set up, so choosing who sets it up is one of the bigger decisions in the project. Get it right and the system fits how your business works. Get it wrong and you end up with expensive software your team quietly works around.

If you're a Melbourne business looking for a CRM consultant, here's what we'd ask, and what good answers sound like. (Yes, we're a Melbourne CRM consultant ourselves, so read this with that in mind. These are the same questions we'd want a client to ask us.)

## Why local can help

You don't need a consultant in the same city. Most CRM work happens over video calls and screen shares, and we work with clients across Australia and overseas.

That said, being local does help with a few things. Workshops where you map the whole process are often better in person, with the people who actually do the work in the room. Same time zone means faster answers when something goes wrong. And a Melbourne consultant is more likely to know the local tools, suppliers and accountants your business already deals with.

Treat local as a bonus, not the main reason to pick someone.

## 1. How will you learn how our business works?

This is the most important question. A good consultant spends time understanding how leads come in, how quotes get written, who follows up and where information gets typed twice, **before** they configure anything.

Be wary of anyone who goes straight to a demo or a template. The platform should fit your process, not the other way round.

## 2. Which platforms do you work with, and would you recommend something else?

Some consultants only sell one platform, so every answer ends with that platform. Ask what else they work with, and when they'd recommend something different. A consultant who has worked with Zoho, HubSpot, Xero and NetSuite, and can build custom when none of them fit, has less reason to steer you somewhere that doesn't suit you.

A good sign: they're willing to tell you that you don't need a new system yet.

## 3. Can you show us similar work?

Ask for examples from businesses like yours, ideally in your industry or with a similar way of selling. A trade supplier with quoting and purchase orders has very different needs to an online store or a professional services firm.

Ask what changed for that client after go-live, not just what was built.

## 4. Who owns the accounts, the data and the setup?

This one is easy to miss and painful to fix later. Your CRM should be in **your** account, paid for by **you**, with admin access for someone in your business. Integrations, automation and custom code should be documented and handed over.

If a consultant wants to hold the account or keeps the setup to themselves, you'll be locked in to them.

## 5. How will you handle our existing data?

Most businesses have customer data scattered across spreadsheets, email, an old CRM and accounting software. Ask how they'll clean it, merge duplicates and decide what's worth bringing across. Moving messy data into a new system just gives you a more expensive mess.

## 6. What will you connect it to?

A CRM on its own is just a better spreadsheet. The value comes from connecting it to the rest of your business: website forms, accounting software like Xero, email, quoting tools and ad platforms. Ask which integrations they'll set up, and how they'll make sure leads can be traced from the ad or search that brought them in through to the invoice.

## 7. How will you get the team using it?

The most common reason CRM projects fail isn't the software. It's the team not using it. Ask how they'll train people, whether they'll roll out in stages, and what they do when people slip back into old habits.

The best answer usually involves starting with the part of the business that hurts most, getting it working well, and building from there.

## 8. What happens after go-live?

Your business will change, and your CRM will need to change with it. Ask what support looks like after launch: who you call when something breaks, how changes are handled and whether they'll review the setup as you grow.

## On cost

You'll notice we haven't talked about price. Every CRM project is different, and the honest answer depends on how many people use it, how many systems it connects to and how much data needs cleaning. Be careful with fixed-price packages that are quoted before anyone has looked at how your business works. Ask instead for a scoping conversation, and for a quote that explains what's driving the cost.
