Ask any business owner why they don't have more Google reviews and you'll usually hear the same thing: "Our customers are happy, they just never get around to it." That's almost always true. The problem isn't goodwill. It's that leaving a review takes too many steps, and nobody asked at the right time.

The fix is simple, and it's free. You need a link that opens your review box in one tap, a habit of sending it the day you finish, and a QR code wherever customers stand around with their phone in hand. We built a free [Google review link and QR code generator](/tools/google-review-qr) that makes all three in a couple of minutes. This guide covers how to use them well.

## Why reviews matter more than you think

Reviews do two jobs. They help you show up: Google's own guidance says the number of reviews and your ratings play a part in how local businesses rank in Maps and the local results. And they help you win the click once you do show up, because people compare the businesses side by side and pick the one with recent, specific reviews.

Recent matters as much as the total. A business with 40 reviews, the newest from last week, usually looks more trustworthy than one with 120 that stopped two years ago. That's why a steady habit beats a one-off push.

## Step 1: Get a link that opens the review box

When you tell a customer to "find us on Google", you're asking them to search, pick the right listing, find the reviews tab and then the button. Plenty of them stop halfway.

A direct review link skips all of that. Google gives you one in your Business Profile:

1. Sign in to your Google Business Profile, or search your business name on Google while signed in to the account that manages it.
2. Choose **Ask for reviews** (sometimes shown as "Get more reviews").
3. Copy the link. It looks like `g.page/r/…/review`.

If someone else manages your profile, or you can't find the button, you can build the same link from your Place ID instead. Paste either into the [free generator](/tools/google-review-qr) and it gives you the finished link, checks it, and lets you test it.

Always press the link yourself before you send it to anyone. It should open your business with the star rating box ready.

## Step 2: Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is the same day the job is finished, while the customer is still pleased and remembers the details. Leave it a fortnight and the moment has passed.

What works for most small businesses:

- **Trades and services:** a text the afternoon you finish, or when you send the final invoice.
- **Shops, cafés and clinics:** a card or stand at the counter, plus a follow-up email if you have their address.
- **Online and project work:** an email at sign-off, after they've said they're happy.

Ask every customer, not just the ones you think loved it. That's Google's rule (more below), and it's also better for you: a handful of honest four-star reviews makes your five-star ones more believable.

## Step 3: Keep the message short and personal

You don't need clever copy. You need their name, a thank you, the reason it matters, and the link. Something like:

> Hi Sarah, thanks for choosing Smith & Sons! If you have a minute, we'd really appreciate a quick Google review. It helps other locals find us: g.page/r/…/review

That's it. The generator writes a text and an email version with your business name and link already filled in, so you can copy them into your phone or job software.

If your system sends invoices or job-completion emails automatically, add the review link there once and it goes out every time without anyone remembering. (That's the kind of small automation we set up for clients all the time.)

## Step 4: Put a QR code where people wait

A QR code does the same job as the link for people standing in front of you. Point the phone camera at it, tap, review. Good spots:

- On the counter, at the register or on the table
- In the waiting room or on the back of the door
- On printed invoices, receipts and quotes
- On your business cards and job sheets
- On the van, if people see it parked at the job

The generator gives you a ready-made review card (four to an A4 sheet, so you can print and cut them yourself) plus the QR code on its own as a PNG for screens and an SVG for your printer or sign-writer. Keep it dark on white, at least 2 cm wide, and test a print with two different phones before you order a batch.

## Step 5: Reply to every review

Replying takes a minute and does more than people expect. A reply shows future customers you're real and you care, it turns a lukewarm review into a good impression, and it reminds the reviewer why they chose you.

Thank people by name and mention the job if you can ("glad the new deck came up well"). For a negative review, reply calmly, once, and offer to sort it out offline. Never argue in public. Readers judge the reply more than the complaint.

## Stay within the rules

This part matters. Google's policies don't allow:

- **Incentives:** discounts, freebies or prize draws in exchange for a review.
- **Review gating:** only sending the link to customers you think are happy, or screening them first with a "how did we do?" step.
- **Fake reviews:** writing your own, or having staff, family or a paid service write them.

Google can remove reviews or restrict your profile for these. In Australia, the ACCC also treats fake and incentivised reviews as misleading conduct under consumer law. Asking every customer for an honest review is completely fine, and it's all you need to do.

## Make it a habit, not a campaign

The businesses with great review profiles aren't doing anything special. They ask every customer, the same way, every time, and they reply. Set it up once:

1. Make your link, QR code and cards with the [free generator](/tools/google-review-qr).
2. Save the text message as a template on your phone or in your job software.
3. Put the cards where customers wait.
4. Check your reviews weekly and reply to each one.
