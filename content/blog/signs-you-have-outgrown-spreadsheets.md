Almost every business we work with started on spreadsheets, and that's the right call. They're flexible, free and everyone knows how to use them. The trouble is that they don't tell you when you've outgrown them. You just slowly start losing time, money and customers.

## The seven signs

**1. There are multiple versions of the truth.** Pricing_FINAL.xlsx, Pricing_FINAL_v2.xlsx and the copy on someone's laptop. Nobody's sure which is current.

**2. One person knows how it works.** If the person who built the formulas goes on holiday and everything slows down, that's a business risk, not a spreadsheet.

**3. Quoting takes too long.** Looking up supplier prices, calculating margins and copying it all into a template eats hours that should be spent selling.

**4. Leads fall through the cracks.** Enquiries live in inboxes, phones and sticky notes. Follow-ups depend on someone remembering.

**5. You type the same thing twice.** Orders get entered in one place, then again in Xero, then again in an email to the supplier. Every retype is a chance for a mistake.

**6. Simple questions take hours.** "What's our margin on that product line?" or "Which customers haven't ordered in six months?" should take seconds.

**7. Mistakes are reaching customers.** Wrong prices, missed orders, double bookings. By the time customers notice, it's costing you more than money.

## CRM or ERP: what's the difference?

A **CRM** (customer relationship management) system looks after the front of the business: leads, customers, quotes, follow-ups and your sales pipeline. An **ERP** (enterprise resource planning) system runs the back: stock, purchasing, orders, fulfilment and finance. Plenty of small businesses need a bit of both, and platforms like Zoho, HubSpot, Xero and NetSuite cover different parts of that picture.

When none of them fit how you work, a custom system can. That's exactly why we built [Tradeflow](/products/tradeflow): small suppliers kept telling us they were stuck between spreadsheets and enterprise software.

## How to make the move without chaos

- **Map how work actually happens** before choosing any software. The tool should fit the process, not the other way round.
- **Clean your data first.** Moving messy spreadsheets into a new system just gives you a more expensive mess.
- **Move in stages.** Start with the part that hurts most, usually quoting or lead tracking, and get it working before adding more.
- **Train the team properly.** A system nobody uses is just another spreadsheet with a login.
- **Make sure you own it.** Your data, your accounts, documented properly, so you're never locked in to whoever set it up.
