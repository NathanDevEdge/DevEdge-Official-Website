Search "how much does an app cost" and you'll find price ranges so wide they're useless. That's not because agencies are being cagey. It's because "app" covers everything from a quoting calculator to a two-sided marketplace. A number without a scope is a guess.

So instead of another made-up range, here's what actually drives the cost of a custom app, and how to get a quote you can hold someone to.

## The five things that move the price

**1. Screens and user flows.** Every screen needs designing, building and testing. More important than the number of screens is the number of journeys: sign up, place an order, approve a quote, request a refund. Each journey has edge cases, and edge cases are where the time goes.

**2. User roles and permissions.** An app where everyone sees the same thing is simple. An app where customers, staff, managers and suppliers each see different data and can do different things is not. Roles touch every screen.

**3. Integrations.** Connecting to Xero, a CRM like Zoho or HubSpot, a payment provider or a carrier's API is often what makes an app genuinely useful. Each integration adds work, and some third-party APIs are a lot friendlier than others.

**4. Platforms.** A web app runs everywhere with one codebase. Native iOS and Android apps can do more on a phone (offline use, the camera, push notifications), but you're shipping to two app stores. Cross-platform frameworks narrow the gap, but they don't close it.

**5. Reporting and admin.** The part customers never see is often the part your team uses most: dashboards, exports, settings, the ability to fix a customer's order without calling a developer. It's easy to forget and expensive to bolt on later.

## The costs after launch

Software isn't finished when it launches. Budget for hosting, app store fees, security updates, keeping up with new iOS and Android versions, and the improvements you'll want once real users get their hands on it. ## How to keep the cost down

- **Start with the smallest useful version.** Build the one journey that matters most, put it in front of real users, then grow it.
- **Don't rebuild what exists.** If a platform like Zoho already does 80% of what you need, configuring and extending it is often smarter than building from scratch.
- **Question the native app.** Plenty of "apps" work better as a fast web app that staff and customers open in a browser.
- **Write the scope down.** Vague scopes are the number one cause of blown budgets, on both sides.
