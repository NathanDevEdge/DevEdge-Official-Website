Whether you're building a new deck or replacing tired boards on an old one, the first question is always the same: how many boards do I actually need? Order too few and the job stops halfway while you wait on the timber yard. Order too many and you've paid for a stack of offcuts.

The good news is that the maths is simple once you know the steps. This guide walks through it in plain English, with a worked example. If you'd rather skip the maths, our free [decking calculator](/tools/decking-calculator) does it for you, including joists, bearers, screws and clips.

## Step 1: Measure the deck and decide which way the boards run

You need two numbers in metres: the **length** of the deck the way the boards run, and the **width** across the boards.

Which way the boards run matters, because it changes how many rows you need and how long each one is. Most decks run the boards along the longer side, often at right angles to the house so rain drains off the ends. If your deck is 6 m by 3.6 m and the boards run along the 6 m side, each row is 6 m long and the rows stack up across 3.6 m.

## Step 2: Count the rows of boards

Each row of boards takes up the width of one board plus one gap. So divide the width across the boards by that, and round up:

**Rows = width across the boards ÷ (board width + gap)**

Use millimetres for this step. A 90 mm board with a 5 mm gap takes up 95 mm per row. A 140 mm board with a 4 mm gap takes up 144 mm.

Use the face width of the board, the part you see on top, not the nominal size on the docket. And if the last row only needs a thin sliver of board, plan to rip the first and last boards to the same width so the deck looks even.

## Step 3: Turn rows into lineal metres

Timber yards sell decking by the lineal metre (the total length of board), so multiply:

**Lineal metres = rows × length of each row**

As a rule of thumb, 90 mm boards with a 5 mm gap need about **10.5 lineal metres per square metre** of deck. 140 mm boards with a 4 mm gap need about **6.9 lineal metres per square metre**. Wider boards mean fewer rows, fewer screws and a faster install.

## Step 4: Add a waste allowance

Some board always ends up as offcuts, split ends, a twisted board you won't use, or a cut that goes wrong. Allow:

- **10%** for a simple rectangular deck
- **15% or more** for angles, steps, curves, picture framing or boards laid on a diagonal

## Step 5: Turn metres into boards

If you're buying set lengths (5.4 m and 6.0 m are common), this is where it pays to think, because the board length can change your order more than the waste allowance does.

- **If a board is longer than a row**, you get one row out of each board, or two if the row is less than half the board's length. The rest is offcut.
- **If a board is shorter than a row**, every row needs a join. Each join has to land on a joist, so you'll want to stagger them and often double up the joists there.

The best length is one that matches your deck, or divides into it neatly.

## A worked example

Here's a 6 m by 3.6 m deck, with 140 mm boards and a 4 mm gap, running along the 6 m length:

1. **Rows:** 3,600 mm ÷ 144 mm = 25 rows.
2. **Lineal metres:** 25 rows × 6 m = 150 m.
3. **With 10% waste:** 165 lineal metres.
4. **In 6.0 m boards:** one board per row, so 25 boards, or **28 boards** with waste. No joins at all.
5. **In 5.4 m boards:** each row needs a full board plus a 0.6 m piece, so 28 boards, or **31 boards** with waste, and a join in every row.

Same deck, same metres on paper, but the 5.4 m boards mean three more boards and 25 joins to line up on joists. That's why it's worth checking before you order.

## What about joists, bearers and screws?

The boards sit on a frame, and the same approach works for it:

- **Joists** run under the boards at right angles to them. Divide the length of the deck by the joist spacing, round up and add one. At 450 mm centres, our 6 m deck needs 15 joists, each 3.6 m long.
- **Bearers** sit under the joists, running the same way as the boards. Divide the width by the bearer spacing, round up and add one.
- **Screws:** two in every board at every joist it crosses. For our example that's 25 rows × 15 joists × 2 = 750 screws, plus a few spare for the ones that drop through the gaps. As a guide, 90 mm boards on 450 mm joists take about 47 screws per square metre, and 140 mm boards take about 31.
- **Hidden clips** go in every gap at every joist, plus a starter and end clip, so you need roughly one per board per joist instead of two screws.

Joist spacing depends on the board. **450 mm** centres is standard for 19 mm hardwood, but most composite boards need **350 to 400 mm**, and diagonal boards need closer joists again. Bearer and post spacing depend on the size of your timber, so check the span tables (AS 1684) or ask your supplier. This guide is for ordering, not for engineering your frame.
