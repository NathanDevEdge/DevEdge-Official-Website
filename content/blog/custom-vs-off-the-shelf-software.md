## The SaaS trap

Off-the-shelf software is fantastic for the things every business does the same way: email, payroll, basic accounting. But when it comes to the part of your operation that makes you different, generic tools push you into generic workflows. The tell is the spreadsheet sitting next to the software, quietly doing the job the software can't.

## When off-the-shelf is the right call

If your processes are standard for your industry, your team is small and the tool covers most of what you need, buy it. It's quick to set up, someone else maintains it, and you can get on with running the business. We'll tell you this ourselves when it's true.

## The middle option most businesses miss

It's rarely a straight choice between "buy" and "build". Platforms like Zoho, HubSpot, Xero and NetSuite can be configured, extended and connected far beyond what most businesses use them for. Custom fields, automations and integrations often close the gap for a fraction of the effort of a full build. That's a big part of our [CRM & ERP Systems](/services/crm-erp-systems) work.

## When to build custom

**1. It's your competitive advantage.** If the way you quote, schedule or serve customers is why you win work, you can't run it on the same tool as everyone else.

**2. You're living in workarounds.** Exports, re-imports, copy and paste between systems. Every workaround is a cost you pay every day.

**3. Per-user pricing is scaling against you.** Subscriptions that made sense for five people can look very different at fifty, and you're paying for features you don't use.

**4. Your tools don't talk to each other.** A custom system, or custom integrations, can make your existing tools work as one.

**5. You want to own it.** Custom software is an asset. You control the roadmap, the data and the pace of change.

## Questions to ask before you decide

- What are we doing outside our current software, and how often?
- Could our existing platform do this with the right setup?
- What would it cost us to keep doing it the current way for another two years?
- Who will look after a custom system once it's built?
